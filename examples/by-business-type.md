# Match to a business type

## The task

You know what your business does. You do not know a single competitor by name, you do not know which
category you belong to in anyone's classification, and you do not have a keyword worth searching.

That is enough to start. Of the four recipes here, this one asks the least of you: name what you do,
name where you are, and the advertising of businesses like yours comes back.

## Ask your assistant

> I run a [business type] in [country]. Show me advertising from businesses like mine — what they
> say, what the images and videos look like, and where they send people.

When something in the answer is worth returning to:

> Save the ten most interesting of those to a folder called [name].

## What comes back

Advertising from businesses that do roughly what you do, in the market you named. For each one: the
words they use, the images and videos a person actually sees, and the page the advertising sends
people to.

The folder request puts your picks somewhere you can open again later, from any client, without
searching for them a second time.

## How to read it

**Read the words before you look at the pictures.** How other businesses in your line describe
themselves — what they lead with, what they leave out — is the fastest read on what customers in
your market respond to.

**Watch what repeats.** One business making a particular claim is one business. Eleven of them
making it is a pattern in your category, and worth a decision either way: join it, or deliberately
stand apart from it.

**Look at where people are sent.** A short form, a long page, a price list, a booking calendar —
the destination tells you what the offer actually is.

**Then decide for yourself.** This shows you what businesses like yours are advertising. It does not
tell you what you should run, and it does not know your margins, your capacity or your reputation.
That part is yours.

## Who this is for

- **A small or medium business** that cannot name a competitor but can name what it does.
- **A content creator** working out which approaches hold up in their line of work.
- **An agency** in the opening conversation with a new client, in an industry they do not know yet.
- **A brand manager** landing in a market that is new to them.
