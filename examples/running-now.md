# What's running right now in an industry

## The task

You need the state of a whole category, not a single competitor — what is new and picking up, what
has been running for a while, and what changed. The kind of picture that normally takes an analyst
a week.

## Ask your assistant

> What's working in [industry] advertising right now in [country]? Show me what's new and picking
> up, and what has been running for a while.

Then put a time axis on it:

> Compare that with three months ago — what changed?

## What comes back

Two halves of the same picture. The new end: what has recently started picking up in the industry
and country you named. The settled end: what has been running long enough to count as established.

The follow-up gives you movement — what has appeared since, what has held, what has gone.

## How to read it

**Read the two halves against each other.** New material clustering around a theme that was absent
three months ago is a category shifting. New material that looks like the established material is a
category that is stable and crowded. Those are different situations and they call for different
decisions.

**"Working" here means running and holding.** It is about what is being paid for and kept on air.
It is not a claim about anyone's profit, and no one outside the advertiser can make that claim
honestly.

**Look at what disappeared.** Comparing across three months makes absences visible, and an approach
that was everywhere and is now gone is as informative as one that arrived.

**Use it to ask better questions, not to reach conclusions.** This is the view from above. When
something in it looks important, go down a level: pull the accounts behind it, or the individual ads.

## Who this is for

- **A head of marketing** who wants the category in one request instead of a week of analyst time.
- **An agency** getting up to speed in a client's industry quickly.
- **A small business** that wants to know what its industry is doing while it still knows nobody by
  name.
