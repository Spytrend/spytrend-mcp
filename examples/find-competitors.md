# Find who else is advertising in your market

## The task

You are about to spend money advertising in a market, and you want to know who is already spending
money there — including the ones you have never heard of.

This is the question you cannot answer by listing the competitors you already know. The usual
mistake when entering a new country or category is to go looking for familiar names, and the
familiar names are rarely the ones doing the volume.

## Ask your assistant

> I sell [what you sell] in [country]. Who else is advertising in this space right now, and what
> are they running? Show me who is behind the ads, not just the single ads.

If the answer comes back too broad, narrow it in one more step:

> Take the three biggest of those and show me everything each of them runs — pages, domains,
> countries and how long each ad has been up.

## What comes back

From the opening question: the accounts advertising in your space, with a sense of the scale of
each — how much they are running, across how many pages and domains, in which countries.

From the follow-up: for each account you picked, the whole body of work in one place. Every ad,
every video, every page, every domain, every country and every run date. You are not clicking
through tabs assembling it yourself; it arrives assembled.

## How to read it

**Start with scale, not with names.** An account running two hundred ads across six countries is
telling you something different from one running four ads in one city. Sort your attention by
volume before you look at anything else.

**Then look at how long things have been up.** An ad that has been running for months has been paid
for repeatedly. That is a fact about spending, not a claim about profit — but a spender who keeps
paying is worth understanding.

**Then look at where people are sent.** The pages and domains behind an account's ads say more about
how the business works than the ads themselves do.

**Finally, look at what you did not expect.** The value in this answer is usually not the competitor
you already had on your list.

## Who this is for

- **A brand manager** who needs to put a picture in front of leadership, not a folder of
  screenshots.
- **A head of marketing** deciding whether to enter a category or a country at all.
- **A competitive research analyst**, for whom doing this by hand is hours per competitor.
- **An agency** that would rather bill for a competitor review than hand one over free before the
  contract is signed.
