# Top ads in one market

## The task

You know the market. Now you want to see what is running in it, ordered by the two things that can
actually be measured: how long each ad has been up, and how many times it has been put back into
rotation.

**What "top" means here.** It means top by run time and re-launches. It does not mean top by
revenue, and nothing on this page claims otherwise — an ad that has been running a long time has
been paid for repeatedly, and that is the fact. What that spend earned is not something anyone
outside the advertiser can see.

## Ask your assistant

> Show me the ads running in [market or category] in [country]. Sort them by how long they have been
> running and how many times they have been run again, and tell me what they have in common.

To go from the list to the reasons behind it:

> Take the five at the top and show me the images and videos in full. What do they share?

## What comes back

The ads running in the market you named, with each one's run dates and how many times it has been
run again, ordered so the long-lived ones surface. The assistant's read on what they have in common
comes on top of that — the raw material is there for you to disagree with.

## How to read it

**Long run time is a spending signal, not a profit signal.** Someone kept paying. That is worth
knowing, and it is all it is.

**Re-launches are the more interesting number.** An ad that has been switched off and put back on
several times was chosen again each time, by someone with numbers you cannot see.

**Look for the shape underneath, not the surface.** An ad on its own is the visible end of an offer,
an audience and a budget you cannot see, so the surface is the least useful part of it. What is
worth studying is the structure: what these ads lead with, what they promise, what they leave out,
how quickly they get to the point. That is what you study before you build your own.

**Check the age of the top ads against your own.** If the long-lived ads in your category are
several months old and yours turn over weekly, that difference is the finding.

## Who this is for

- **A media buyer** who wants to see an approach while it is still working, and know when it is
  worn out.
- **A content creator or social media manager** looking for what holds up in a category.
- **A brand manager** who wants to know what has staying power in their market rather than what
  launched last week.
